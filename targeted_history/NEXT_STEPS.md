## What to do next

1. **Finish Bengal's historical body review.** There are 255 usable Bengal-related candidate articles still awaiting full-body reading. These are location/desk-screened candidates, not 255 verified Bengal incidents. Separate ordinary crime, custody/reconstruction, border operations, crowds and insurgency.
2. **Build the closest-comparison table.** Record custody status, alleged firearm-taking, individual/group attribution, reconstruction, who was shot, outcome and incident date. Deduplicate repeated reports. Keep broader similarities and unresolved sources explicit.
3. **Independently check the strongest matches.** Open the linked originals and check the coding. Record a correction with the case ID, source URL and the passage that changes your assessment.
4. **Reassess Bengal before and after May 2026.** Apply the same retrieval and coding to comparator states before estimating differences. Sparse or flat pre-period outcomes and changing coverage can leave synthetic comparisons poorly determined. Selected existence examples supply no event-rate denominator.

## What to check in each report

- Distinguish incident date from publication/update date.
- Check who allegedly took whose weapon, whether the taking was attempted or completed, whether the claim concerns an individual or a group, who fired, and whether the person was already in custody.
- Code reconstruction only when the report establishes it. Keep ordinary crime, insurgency, border confrontations, crowds and jail allegations separate.
- Check the chief minister and governing party or coalition **at the incident date**, including historical party names. Later party affiliations should not be backdated.
- Keep state government, coalition participation and force authority separate. Delhi Police, BSF and CISF require Union-authority context; Sitalkuchi also involves election supervision. Incumbency does not establish political orders or causation.
